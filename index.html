LGBTQ+ & Flag Meanings

Pride flags are powerful symbols of identity, community, and resistance. Here are some of the key flags you should know:

Progress Pride Flag

Designed by Daniel Quasar in 2018. It includes five arrow-shaped stripes representing trans individuals, people of color, and those living with or lost to HIV/AIDS.

Non-Binary Flag

Created by Kye Rowan in 2014. Yellow represents gender outside the binary, white represents many/all genders, purple represents a mix of male and female, and black represents agender/no gender.

Transgender Flag

Created by Monica Helms in 1999. Light blue represents traditional boys' color, pink represents traditional girls' color, and white represents those transitioning or non-binary.

Bisexual Flag

Designed by Michael Page in 1998. Pink stands for attraction to the same gender, blue for attraction to a different gender, and purple for attraction to more than one gender.

Understanding Gender Identities

Skin Tone Diversity & Anti-Racism

Understanding human diversity requires addressing how society treats people based on skin color and racial categorization.

Colorism

Prejudice or discrimination against individuals with a dark skin tone, typically among people of the same ethnic or racial group. Anti-racism work requires unlearning colorist biases.

Systemic Racism

Policies and practices that exist throughout a whole society or organization, and that result in and support a continued unfair advantage to some people and unfair or harmful treatment of others based on race.

Intersectionality

A term coined by Kimberlé Crenshaw. It describes how different forms of discrimination (like racism, sexism, and homophobia) overlap and intersect, creating unique experiences of oppression.
